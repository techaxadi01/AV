📐 Baudhayana (Pythagorean) Triplets

Three whole numbers that fit a right-angled triangle perfectly.

1. The big idea

Around 800 BCE, the Indian mathematician Baudhayana wrote in his Sulba Sutra that in a right-angled triangle, the square on the longest side (hypotenuse) equals the sum of the squares on the other two sides. Europe later called it the Pythagoras theorem.

a² + b² = c²  (c is the longest side)

A Baudhayana triplet is a set of three whole numbers (a, b, c) that satisfy this. Example: 3, 4, 5 because 9 + 16 = 25.

Fun fact: builders used a rope with 12 equal knots, bent into 3 + 4 + 5, to make a perfect right angle.

2. See it: squares on the sides

Pick a way to choose the triangle. The areas of the blue and green squares always add up to the red one. A triplet appears only when all three sides are whole numbers.

3. Rule 1: calculate a triplet from m

Rule 1 (class 8 textbook rule): for any natural number m > 1,

(2m, m² − 1, m² + 1) is a triplet

Why it works: (m²+1)² − (m²−1)² = 4m² = (2m)². So (2m)² + (m²−1)² = (m²+1)². ✔

4. Rule 2: more triplets using m and n

Rule 1 only uses one number. Rule 2 uses two whole numbers, so it gives many more triplets. Pick whole numbers m > n (both at least 1):

(m² − n², 2mn, m² + n²)

Rule 1 is Rule 2 with n = 1. Example: m = 3, n = 2 gives (5, 12, 13). If m and n have no common factor and one is even, you get a “primitive” triplet that can’t be shrunk.

Why it works: (m² + n²)² − (m² − n²)² = 4m²n² = (2mn)². So (m² − n²)² + (2mn)² = (m² + n²)². ✔

Worked example: m = 5, n = 2 → m² − n² = 25 − 4 = 21, 2mn = 2×5×2 = 20, m² + n² = 25 + 4 = 29. Check: 21² + 20² = 441 + 400 = 841 = 29². ✔

5. Properties of Baudhayana triplets

  1. Scaling: if (a, b, c) is a triplet, then (ka, kb, kc) is also a triplet for any natural number k. From (3, 4, 5) we get (6, 8, 10), (9, 12, 15), (12, 16, 20) …
  2. The hypotenuse is the largest: c is always the biggest number. Also, the smallest number that can appear in any triplet is 3.
  3. At least one leg is even, so the area ½ × a × b is always a whole number. Example: (5, 12, 13) has area ½ × 5 × 12 = 30.
  4. Odd and even: in a primitive triplet (no common factor), one leg is odd, the other leg is even, and the hypotenuse is odd. Example: (8, 15, 17).
  5. Divisibility: in every triplet, one number is a multiple of 3, one is a multiple of 4 and one is a multiple of 5 (sometimes one number does two jobs). So a × b × c is always a multiple of 60. Example: (5, 12, 13) → 12 is a multiple of 3 and 4, and 5 is a multiple of 5.
  6. Rule 1 triplets: the hypotenuse is always 2 more than the longer leg, because (m² + 1) − (m² − 1) = 2. Example: (8, 15, 17) → 17 − 15 = 2.
  7. Every odd number from 3 is in a triplet: for odd n, use (n, (n² − 1)/2, (n² + 1)/2). Example: n = 7 → (7, 24, 25). Every even number from 4 is in a Rule 1 triplet too: n = 2m.
  8. Converse: if three sides satisfy a² + b² = c², the triangle must be right-angled. This is why the 3-4-5 rope makes a perfect right angle.

5B. Triplet ki properties (Hinglish mein)

Har property ek alag box mein hai, aur uske neeche ek example bhi hai.

Property 1: Guna karne par bhi triplet rehta hai (Scaling)

Agar (a, b, c) triplet hai, to (ka, kb, kc) bhi triplet hoga, jahan k koi bhi natural number ho.

Example: (3, 4, 5) × 2 = (6, 8, 10) → 36 + 64 = 100 ✔
(3, 4, 5) × 3 = (9, 12, 15) → 81 + 144 = 225 ✔

Property 2: Hypotenuse sabse bada hota hai

c (hypotenuse) hamesha teeno numbers mein sabse bada hota hai. Aur kisi bhi triplet mein sabse chhota number 3 hota hai, 1 ya 2 se triplet ban hi nahi sakta.

Example: (3, 4, 5) mein 5 sabse bada hai, isliye 5 hypotenuse hai. 3² + 4² = 5² ✔

Property 3: Ek leg hamesha even hoti hai

Isliye triangle ka area ½ × a × b hamesha whole number aata hai.

Example: (5, 12, 13) mein 12 even hai. Area = ½ × 5 × 12 = 30 (whole number) ✔

Property 4: Odd-even ka pattern (primitive triplet mein)

Jis triplet mein teeno numbers ka koi common factor nahi hota, usme ek leg odd, ek leg even aur hypotenuse odd hota hai.

Example: (8, 15, 17) → 8 even, 15 odd, 17 odd ✔
(20, 21, 29) → 20 even, 21 odd, 29 odd ✔

Property 5: 3, 4 aur 5 ke multiples

Har triplet mein ek number 3 ka multiple, ek number 4 ka multiple aur ek number 5 ka multiple hota hai (kabhi kabhi ek hi number do kaam kar deta hai). Isliye a × b × c hamesha 60 ka multiple hota hai.

Example: (5, 12, 13) → 12 = 3 aur 4 dono ka multiple, 5 = 5 ka multiple.
Product = 5 × 12 × 13 = 780 = 60 × 13 ✔

Property 6: Rule 1 wale triplet mein c − b = 2

(2m, m² − 1, m² + 1) mein hypotenuse aur lambi leg ka difference hamesha 2 hota hai, kyunki (m² + 1) − (m² − 1) = 2.

Example: m = 4 → (8, 15, 17), aur 17 − 15 = 2 ✔
m = 5 → (10, 24, 26), aur 26 − 24 = 2 ✔

Property 7: Har odd number (3 se shuru) kisi triplet mein aata hai

Agar n odd number hai, to triplet hoga: (n, (n² − 1)/2, (n² + 1)/2). Even number n (4 se shuru) ke liye Rule 1 mein n = 2m rakho.

Example: n = 7 → (49 − 1)/2 = 24 aur (49 + 1)/2 = 25, yani (7, 24, 25) → 49 + 576 = 625 ✔
n = 6 → m = 3 → (6, 8, 10) ✔

Property 8: Ulta bhi sach hai (Converse)

Agar kisi triangle ki sides ke liye a² + b² = c² ho, to wo triangle zaroor right-angled hoga. Isi wajah se 3-4-5 wali rope se perfect right angle banta hai.

Example 1: sides 5, 12, 13 → 25 + 144 = 169 = 13², to right angle hai ✔
Example 2: sides 4, 5, 6 → 16 + 25 = 41, lekin 6² = 36, to right angle nahi hai ✘
